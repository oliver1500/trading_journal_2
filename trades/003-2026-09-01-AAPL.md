## Lessons learned

- **Outcome was good, exit process wasn't.** The exit landed almost exactly at the level AAPL had dropped from (~320) — but it was decided by fear reacting to one red SPY candle, not by a level or rule set before entry. No take-profit order was placed; the exit was purely discretionary ("just looking with my eyes").
- **The stop loss itself was handled correctly** — it was never touched or removed while the position was open. It auto-cancelled only after the sell, because a flat position has nothing left to protect; that's normal broker behavior, not a risk-management mistake.
- **The gap was on the profit side, not the protection side**: risk was defined (stop in place throughout), but the upside had no defined target — which is exactly what left the exit decision to be made under pressure, in the moment, instead of in advance.
- **Exit rule should track the actual thesis** (AAPL's relative strength vs SPY breaking down), not a single candle in a different symbol.
- Confirmed in conversation: scalping compresses decision time to seconds, which is exactly where this trade's discipline broke down — a slower timeframe is a better place to build the habit of deciding exits before entries.
